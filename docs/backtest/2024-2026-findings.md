### What this says
1. **No timeframe shows a positive expectancy** for the V1 Buy Limit over 33 months. "+2R before −1R" is below the ~33% break-even on all three.
2. **M15 is negative with reasonable confidence:** its 95% interval excludes zero, and it is negative in every year, getting worse. That is evidence against M15 as the live timeframe.
3. **M30 and H1 are statistically indistinguishable from break-even and from each other.**
   - M30 has the best point estimate (−0.08R) and **twice H1's alert frequency** (about 1 per week vs 1 every 2 weeks).
   - M30's per-year results are also steadier (2025 and 2026 ≈ 0).
4. **H1 frequency on the full data is 0.56 alerts/week**, much less sparse than the 2026-only estimate (0.27/week), because 2026 was an unusually low-signal year for H1 (8 signals vs 30 and 41). Whether about 1 alert every 2 weeks is enough for the product is the owner's call.
5. **Stop slippage is small on average** (realized R ≈ −1.01), but single gaps reached 4.7 pips on H1. Planned risk is a good average estimate, not a cap.

## 3. Other observations
- **The RSI rule is effectively "RSI > 50 and rising":**
  - the recovery branch contributes 7%, 7% and 4% of signals;
  - 30 vs 35 changes M15 by 4 signals (283 → 279) and M30/H1 not at all;
  - D1 is practically moot.
- **Variants (Buy Limit):** none turns any timeframe positive with an interval excluding zero.
  - The best point estimates, H1 `entry_candle_mid` −0.01R and M30 `touch_tol_x2` −0.03R, are within noise.
  - Adopting either after seeing this table would be **overfitting**.
- **Secondary diagnostic (PDF market entry, not decision evidence):**
  - On M15 the market entry does better than the limit (−0.10R vs −0.25R), consistent with **adverse selection**: the M15 limit tends to fill on setups that keep falling.
  - On M30 the two are similar (−0.01R vs −0.08R).
  - On H1 the market entry is worse (−0.23R vs −0.12R).
  - This only helps explain the Buy Limit numbers. It is not an argument for changing production.

## 4. Decision support for the owner (not decisions)
- **Live timeframe (D8), on the Buy Limit evidence:**
  - **M15** is disfavoured: it is negative, confidently so, and in every year.
  - **M30** offers the best balance of frequency (≈1 alert/week) and outcome (≈ break-even).
  - **H1** is similar in outcome, at half the frequency.
  - If a choice must be made now, the evidence points to **M30**, with the caveat that it has **not shown an edge**.
- **The headline matters more than the timeframe choice:** V1 as specified (Ahmad's rules, mechanised, with a Buy Limit entry) has **not demonstrated positive expectancy** on 2024–2026 EUR/USD. As a notification tool it works exactly as designed; as a source of profitable trades it is unproven. The options include:
  1. **Continue to paper/live monitoring on M30**, collecting real alert outcomes and comparing them with the backtest before risking capital.
  2. **Revisit the rules with Ahmad (V1.1)**, e.g. the near-inert RSI rule. Any such change should be designed on 2024–2025 only, then tested **once** on 2026 as a holdout, to avoid fitting to this data.
  3. Both.
- **Data follow-ups:** re-export Nov–Dec 2025 from Exness to fill the 4 missing days, and ideally the whole 2025 export (its day order was scrambled). The current repair is sound; a clean export removes the dependency on it.
